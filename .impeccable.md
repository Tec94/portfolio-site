## Design context

### Users

The portfolio serves two equally important audiences:

- Hiring teams evaluating Jack Cao's product engineering depth, ownership, and
  ability to ship polished work.
- Freelance clients deciding whether Jack can understand a problem, build the
  right solution, and communicate clearly.

Visitors usually arrive in an exploratory state. They need to understand the
quality of the work quickly before they choose a live project, resume request,
inquiry, or call.

### Brand personality

Tactile, precise, playful.

The portfolio presents a product engineer who designs. It should feel warm,
editorial, and carefully handled: expressive enough to be memorable, restrained
enough that the work and decisions remain legible.

### Aesthetic direction

- Use warm paper-like light and dark surfaces expressed with OKLCH tokens.
- Use the approved Workshop typography: Besley for display and Geologica for
  body and interface copy.
- Keep the top sparse. Primary navigation lives in a bottom-center dock; theme
  and opt-in interface sound live in a bottom-right utility stack.
- Use restrained editorial rules, framed project media, and shallow paper
  texture. Color belongs primarily to project work and focused accents.
- Selected work combines a bounded spatial sequence with loose framed image
  stacks. The complete archive remains list-first with an optional showcase
  view.
- Use the approved raster cursor as a region-aware enhancement. It responds to
  intent, surface tone, and velocity without positional lag.
- Use Cuelume at 22 percent for semantic interface cues and the uisfx Zen pack
  at a slightly higher level for typing. Expansion uses Cuelume's whisper cue.

### Design principles

1. Put the work first. Animation frames and explains projects, but it does not
   compete with them.
2. Make motion spatially coherent. Scroll direction, media depth, and framed
   stacks must feel connected rather than decorative.
3. Use spectacle selectively. Spatial work treatment, tactile disclosure, and
   sensory cues should not compete with one another.
4. Adapt the structure across breakpoints. Desktop may use a bounded sticky
   showcase; mobile uses ordinary vertical depth and document scrolling.
5. Keep every state honest and accessible. Give keyboard, reduced-motion,
   loading, error, and empty states the same care as the default view.

### Anti-goals

- Generic neon cyberpunk styling, excessive glass panels, gradient text, or
  decorative charts.
- Flashy ambient animation, global smooth scrolling, background music, or
  background WebGL.
- Dense top navigation, oversized section headings, or helper copy that merely
  repeats a label.
- A horizontal mobile reel or a shuffled archive that obscures the approved
  selected-work order.
- Equal-card grids on every page.
- Fake contact success, dead links, or controls that only work with a mouse.
